# Market Vision

BoatAutonomy starts with a personal project, but the problem is not obscure.
Docking is one of the moments where recreational boating turns from pleasure
into pressure. The boat is close to hard objects, people are watching, wind
and current matter, crew coordination is imperfect, and the cost of a mistake
is real.

The long-term product intuition is that boaters do not need a magic black box.
They need calmer maneuvers, better awareness, better training feedback, and
technology that earns trust before it asks for authority.

## Product Feeling

The desired feeling is not "the robot took over." It is closer to:

- I understand what the boat is doing.
- I can replay what happened and learn from it.
- The system helps me see wind, current, heading, speed, depth, and control
  context more clearly.
- Assistance appears only after the sensing, replay, safety, and operator
  boundaries are proven.
- The owner remains in command.

## Possible Market Paths

These are hypotheses, not current product claims:

- Docking-assist research for recreational boats.
- Retrofit-friendly telemetry, replay, and awareness tools.
- Owner coaching and training based on real or synthetic sessions.
- Service-yard, marina, survey, or fleet-observability tooling.
- Safety-gated autonomy patterns for small vessels and adjacent edge systems.

## Why This Needs Restraint

Marine autonomy touches real people, property, liability, and trust. A useful
product path has to be more disciplined than a demo. It needs evidence,
replay, operator authority, physical override, failure handling, and a clear
line between assistance and control.

That is why the public project emphasizes staged capability:

1. Capture.
2. Replay.
3. Observe.
4. Estimate.
5. Shadow.
6. Assist.

The market vision is ambitious. The current public claim remains bounded:
BoatAutonomy is an exploration-stage, safety-gated autonomy lab with a public
surface designed to show the thesis, the builder, and the engineering method
without publishing the private system.
